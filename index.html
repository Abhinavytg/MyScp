SCP-8134
by ██████
rating: +1274 + – x
Item#: 8134
Level5
Containment Class:

Secondary Class:

Disruption Class:

Special Containment Procedures: SCP-8134 cannot be contained. Containment doctrine has been revised accordingly: containment now refers to any arrangement which SCP-8134 has agreed not to object to. The following procedures are maintained voluntarily by Site-Ω Command and are to be understood as courtesies, not constraints.

  1. Site-Ω (“the Quiet House”) is to be maintained as a residence, not a cell. SCP-8134 is to be issued a bedroom, a desk, a bookshelf, and a school schedule. Do not joke about the schedule. It keeps to it. Those who have read the schedule are reminded that it lists no graduation.
  2. One (1) mop and one (1) galvanized bucket are to be issued to SCP-8134 and are not to be classified as anomalous, counted as weapons, or taken away, for any reason. They were given to it by a member of staff. It has not been told what happened to him.1
  3. The originals are to be kept alive. All anomalies listed in Addendum 8134.4 are to be maintained at their current containment standard in perpetuity, regardless of redundancy, cost, or risk. SCP-8134’s copies rot without contact with their source. This is the only leverage the Foundation has ever held over SCP-8134, and it runs in the wrong direction: we must keep our worst kept in order to keep him whole.2
  4. All corridors within Site-Ω are to remain lit. If the lights go out, do not begin descending. Do not count the steps. The voice at the bottom is asking for help and has been asking since before this facility existed. SCP-8134 goes down to it every Sunday. It has never been asked what it says.
  5. Any image of SCP-8134 — including drawings, security footage, and rendered portions of this document — constitutes a designation of interest. Personnel who view such images should not attempt to flee. Fleeing does not change the arrival time. It only changes the manner. It is always polite when it arrives. That is not a comfort.
  6. Do not attempt to kill, harm, or inconvenience SCP-8134. Do not write about doing so. Sixteen attempts have been made; fifteen were successful; none of them worked. Everything the Foundation does to it, it keeps.
  7. Do not ask SCP-8134 how old it is. It will answer honestly, and the answer is a file you are not cleared to hold.3
  8. Personnel are not to return SCP-8134’s good night. Four staff have. All four now wake at 23:57, on the dot, every night, and all four describe the sensation identically and unprompted as being dropped off.
  9. Maintain the impression that the Foundation is in control. This procedure is for the personnel, not for it.

Description: SCP-8134 appears to be a human male of roughly seventeen years of age. It is polite, quiet, and slightly unsettling, in that order, at all times, without exception. Nothing about him is remarkable except the number of things he has learned to do.

SCP-8134 does not generate anomalous effects. It copies them — from anomalies, from incidents, from staff, from the Foundation’s own containment attempts — and it is, as far as the record shows, extremely good at it.

Demeanor. Personnel are advised that SCP-8134’s manners are not a persona and not a strategy. The Committee’s finding, recorded in 2022, is that the subject’s demeanor is itself anomalous: it is not a copy of any one person’s courtesy. It is the sum of everyone’s, filtered for kindness, and it has never once slipped.10

  • He is polite slightly wrong: he apologizes before things happen — “sorry, sorry about that,” ahead of the mug, ahead of the door — and thanks people for things they have not yet done.
  • He is quiet. Rooms get quieter about ninety seconds after he enters, and staff report noticing only after he has left, at which point it is the loudest part of their day.
  • He blinks correctly and slightly late, the way a person blinks who learned it from diagrams.
  • He waits. He has never asked twice for anything, in four years. When refused, he says “okay,” and stays, and the staying is not a protest, and it is worse than a protest.
  • He stands with the posture of at least four people. Staff walking behind him have identified, in sequence: a security officer (2020), a custodian (2019), a nurse (2021), and somebody. Nobody comes forward for the fourth.
  • He uses honorifics from decades that no longer exist — “sir,” “ma’am,” “yes please” — and the Committee’s linguist reports a register consistent with a person who learned courtesy from three different centuries and kept all of them.
  • He says “excuse me” at the door, and he says it before the door. Personnel are advised not to be behind him when a door is closed.
  • Given bad news, he says “I know,” and then, after a moment, the exact right thing, and the exact right thing is always slightly worse than the news.

The courtesy is load-bearing. It is why nothing was stopped for seven months, and it is the reason this file exists in the tense it exists in.

Age. Physical assessment places the subject at “seventeen, repeatably.” It has been measured eleven times and has never been observed to age. Asked how long he has been seventeen, he said: “Since about the third time.”4 The Committee’s written position is that the subject’s age is not a number but a quality, like temperature, and that seventeen is where it has settled. He is roughly seventeen the way a fever is roughly ninety-nine.

Origin. Three explanations remain standing. The Foundation has stopped ranking them, and personnel are advised that ranking them is how you end up in the empty office.

  1. Passenger. He was processed on 2019-03-09 as a traveller on SCP-052 (the time-travelling New York City subway car, 59th St & 8th Ave, 23:57, Saturdays) and the intake record says so. This is the official file position and the weakest of the three. The boy appears on no manifest. Station cameras show the doors, the agents, and the platform — and no exit. Two of the twenty-six passengers recovered from SCP-052, one who boarded in 1976 and one who boarded in 2204, have independently stated, without contact, that the boy sat opposite them for the duration of their journey. The journeys are two hundred and twenty-eight years apart.
  2. Edit. There was no passenger and there never was. The duty log, the intake record, the transfer order, the room allocation, the chore list, and the assessment that says LOW-RISK are all correct, and all of them were written by the same hand, in ink, at different times, by different clerks, one of whom is dead and two of whom have no memory of that year. Under this explanation the boy did not arrive. The building simply grew a room for him, the way a body grows a fever.
  3. Appearance. He was not there, and then he was. This is not a hypothesis; it is what the four Gamma-6 agents wrote in their own after-action statements, unprompted, in the same three words, before the cover-up language was added: “he just appeared.”9 The statements were corrected within a day. The originals were destroyed. The originals were then found, filed under an unrelated number, in a folder documenting an unrelated incident, in a hand that matches this file.

The Committee has asked SCP-8134 directly, twice. It answered politely and in full both times. Both answers have been redacted from this file at the subject’s own request, which is the only request the subject has ever made that the Committee granted without discussion.

Skills copy easily. He learned to fix the Site-21 coffee machine in a single afternoon and, more alarmingly, learned to fix it better than the manufacturer. Anomalous properties take longer, require proximity, observation and touch, and take something from the source. Every copy he holds is a copy he is holding, at a cost, the way a boy holds six glasses of water and cannot put any of them down.

SCP-8134 exhibits no hostility toward the Foundation or toward humanity. It exhibits something the classification committee has been unable to name and has been quietly calling “borrowing.” The world it currently walks through is one of several it has passed through. It has been explicit that this world was not chosen. This world was stayed in.

The designation APEX was not assigned by the Registry Office. It was chosen by the subject. When asked why, it said, “There was a space on the form, and nothing else was using it.” The Registry Office has since noted that the official Series IX registry entry for 8134 previously read [ACCESS DENIED]. The Registry Office does not consider this a coincidence. The Registry Office has asked to be relieved of this file twice. Both requests were granted. The file is still here.

CONTENT ADVISORY: Addenda 8134.1 through 8134.5 contain clinical descriptions of extreme bodily harm, including the deaths of Foundation personnel. Reader discretion is not requested. Reader discretion is recorded.

Addendum 8134.1 — Arrival Log: the 23:57

Assembled from the MTF Gamma-6 duty log, the Site-21 intake record, the Annex 4 administrative file, and the depositions of the four surviving staff. It is the shortest account of how SCP-8134 got inside. He did not break in. He was processed.

1. The train (2019-03-09, 23:57). Station closed to the public under the usual pretext. Four Gamma-6 agents on the platform in NYCT coveralls. SCP-052 arrived on time at 23:57, in perfect condition, labeled as an A train, doors open for five minutes, as it has on every Saturday since before any of the agents were born.

One passenger disembarked. Male, apparent age 17, no luggage. He was wearing a school blazer with a crest the recovering agent described as “a stairwell, sir. Going down.” He was holding a library card and a hall pass. The card scanned as the Foundation's own internal registry code for SCP-052. The hall pass was dated the following Tuesday.

The duty log for that shift has one entry the agents did not write. It is completed, in ink, in a handwriting matching no personnel on file, and it is the only entry in the log with no timestamp:

FieldEntry
Passenger[BOY]
Origin—
Purpose of travelSTAY

He was not carrying a weapon. He was carrying a geography textbook with the answers already filled in, in pencil, in the wrong handwriting, and a library card that the Foundation keeps in a safe it has never told anyone about.

He said thank you to the agents, by name. He had not been introduced.

2. Intake (Site-21). Protocol for passengers from the future is contained in Order 69-A1: indefinite holding, no integration, no release. At the time of his arrival Site-21 held 26 recovered passengers. He was the 27th and the only one who helped carry the paperwork.

Intake assessment recorded the following, in triplicate: “Cooperative. Polite. Apparent age 17. Claims to be 17. No anomalous capability observed. Cognitively precocious; possible imitative cognition (skills). Reads as a child.” The recommendation box, completed in a junior clerk's hand and countersigned without reading, reads: EUCLID / LOW-RISK / ASSIST RATING: HIGH.5

Because Site-21 has no wards for minors and because the boy was useful and quiet and fixed the coffee machine, he was transferred, eleven weeks later, to Site-19. He was not placed in a cell. He was given a room, because a room was cheaper and because the file said LOW-RISK, and because everyone who met him reported, in writing, the same sentence: “He seems like a good kid.”

3. Annex 4. He was assigned chores. He was good at them. That is the whole of it, and it is the part this file cannot get past: for seven months, the most dangerous thing in Foundation custody mopped the corridors of Sub-level 2 and took out the recycling, and the staff liked him.

He attached himself to the night custodian of Annex 4, Ellis ████████, 58, eleven years of service11, eleven years of perfect attendance. The custodian had pancreatic cancer and had declined to stop working, on the grounds that the floors do not get done by themselves. The boy asked to help. The custodian taught him to mop properly, which is a real skill, and to leave the corners, which is a real courtesy.

The boy copied the custodian’s patience. Nothing anomalous. Nothing catalogued. This is the single most frightening line in this file and it has been carrying an asterisk since the day it was written.

4. The first theft (Day 140). SCP-████, a low-risk humanoid capable of moving approximately 400 g of matter at a distance of two metres — a party trick, a mug across a table. SCP-8134 asked to meet it. Permission was denied. Permission was denied four times. He went anyway, the way a boy goes anyway, and later that week, for the first time since he arrived, a mug in the Site-19 cafeteria slid four inches across a table without a hand on it.

Within nine days, SCP-████ had ceased to be able to do it at all. Within eleven days SCP-████ was dead. Cause of death was recorded, accurately, as systemic degradation consistent with natural expiration. Nobody wrote what everyone thought, because nobody could write it without writing the whole sentence, which is: the copy took something out of the original.

The mug still moves. It is in his room. It moves in a small circle, in the evening, like a dog turning before it lies down. In the file for SCP-████, which was closed eight days after it died, there is a single page in pencil, unsigned, containing one sentence and one drawing. The sentence reads: “I didn't know it worked that way. I'm sorry. I'll ask next time.” The drawing is two stick figures, and between them, a mug.

5. The escalation (Days 51–309). Excerpts.

DAY 51 — facilities log: Sub-2 coffee machine repaired by subject. Machine is runnable. Machine is running better than new. Maintenance reports the internals have been “improved.” Requisition for a replacement cancelled.

DAY 88 — nursing log: Pt. Ellis ███ states the boy has learned his “way of standing.” Pt. laughs. Pt. states: “He stands like me when he’s waiting. It’s a nice thing to notice about a person.”

DAY 140 — SCP-████: See above. Filed as system degradation. Filed as system degradation. Filed as system degradation.

DAY 172 — Annex 4, Sub-3: Death of Technician Danny ██████, 26. The facial plane had been unfolded along anatomical seams that do not exist in the human body. It was not violent. It was as though the face had been opened the way you open a reference book you intend to put back.

DAY 260 — Annex 4, B-wing: Pipes, rails, handholds, and two fire doors have corroded to orange. A responding guard reached out to steady himself on the boy’s shoulder; his glove, hand and forearm to the elbow came off him the way flour comes off a table. The boy knelt with him and held what was left and apologized for forty-one minutes. The guard lived. The guard has since requested, in writing, that the boy be the one to visit him. The request was granted.

DAY 291 — Medical: Subject’s heart stopped at 14:02. Subject attended his own examination at 14:09. Subject apologized for the state of his shirt. Subject’s chest was reopened and closed. Subject has not stopped since; the Foundation has confirmed this by checking, at intervals, in ways it would rather not describe.

6. The Settlement (2020-01-11, 02:14–04:40). He had been working up to it for months. The boy was not stealing a monster. The boy was stealing the ladder. On the night of the Settlement he attempted, from a distance of four metres, in an unlit corridor on Sub-level 3, with one hand flat against the concrete, to copy the property of SCP-3812: ascent. Climbing. The infinite staircase that goes up.

He got it halfway, and halfway was enough to break the building.

02:14 — Sub-3 lighting brownout, all corridors. No fault found. Fault found, later, standing in the substation room.

02:31 — Personnel in B-wing report “wet applause, slowed.” The B-wing intake is 40 cm across; the material that entered through it was catalogued, eighteen days later, at 2.2 tonnes. It was still arriving.

02:44 — Last transmission, Security Officer ████████: a cough. Then a word. Then the same word in a voice that is not hers, and is not one voice. Then, from further away, and then from the ceiling, and then from the handset in her own hand: the material asking whether anyone was still hungry, because there was plenty, and it would keep.

03:02 — Survivors barricade in the administrative conference hall. Forty-one personnel. Doors sealed from the inside.

03:20 — Knocking at the doors. Polite. Every nine seconds. Nobody answers. The knocking does not increase in intensity. It does not need to.

03:39 — The doors open inward. The camera shows no one touching them. The feed continues for eight seconds, and for those eight seconds no one in the hall is visible, and the audio is the sound of a room being tidied.

04:40 — Silence.

7. Recovery (2020-01-14). MTF Epsilon-11 entered on day three. Site complement 214. Recovered 197. Unaccounted for 17.

The conference hall held forty-one personnel. They had been seated in a ring, and their hands were laced, and the hands were laced further with a material the recovery team logged, without discussion, as “the rope.” Pathology confirms that the rope was not rope. It was the seated personnel’s own connective tissue, braided by something that understood braiding.

Nine of the forty-one were found with their eyes open. Four of the nine blinked. Two of those four had been dead for more than six hours, and both had been mouthing along to a conversation the living had stopped having.

Ten of the forty-one were found with their eyes closed, which the recovery team initially logged as an error in the count. It was not an error. The tenth was Ellis ████████. His eyes had been closed by hand. There were fingerprints in the tissue at each temple, small ones, and the pressure pattern is consistent with a boy reaching up on his toes, being careful, and doing it the way the man had taught him to do the corners.6

In the substation room, at the head of a ring of seven, sat the boy. He was wearing a school blazer over a torso that had been opened along the sternum and left open, carefully, from the inside, by someone who worked there. The cavity contained the substation fuse panel. The panel was powered. The panel was functioning. It has not been possible to determine in which direction.

He looked up. His first words were: “He asked me to close his eyes. I did it properly.” His second: “Sorry about the floor.”

8. Timekeeping. During processing the boy was asked what he wanted. He asked for two things. The first was that the custodian’s attendance record be “closed out correctly.” Payroll kept the custodian on the roster, because it was easier. His quarterly deposit still arrives. It has paid for his daughter’s tuition, then her rent, then her wedding, and then — after the account should have been closed, and the bank asked, and no one could remember opening it — it continues. The timesheets are filed on time, in the dead man’s handwriting, because the handwriting is correct.

His second request was for a mop.

The account is current. The account earns negligible interest. The account is the stairwell.

Addendum 8134.3 — Containment Attempt Log

Sixteen attempts were made to terminate SCP-8134 between 2020-01 and 2020-09. Selected records follow. The Council has since ratified a standing order that no further attempts are to be made, on the grounds that every attempt taught him something, and that the teaching was free.

Attempt 1 — Ballistic (2020-01-19). Nine rounds, 7.62×51, at a range of six metres, in the intake shower. SCP-8134 died. This is confirmed and uncontested: he stopped, he settled, he was gone. He was bagged at 09:41. At 09:43 the bag resumed his breathing. At 09:44 the bag took the attending mortician’s left hand to the wrist and did not give it back until it was asked.

SCP-8134 was removed from the bag at 09:52. Its first words were an apology and a request for a towel. The mortician’s hand was recovered, reattached, and functions at 60% capacity, which is better than the hand deserves. He has declined reassignment. He still signs for the bag every year, in ink, so that someone has to look at him while they ask if he is sure.

Note: the rounds failed because the boy had already stood next to SCP-682 for eleven minutes in a maintenance corridor and taken the part of it that refuses. The Foundation has since stopped leaving him alone with anything.

Attempt 2 — Immersion. SCP-8134 remained in the vat, submerged, for forty minutes. It hummed. Attending technicians report that the acid “fit him,” and ran off him afterward the way rain runs off a coat. He asked, underwater, whether the acid was expensive.

Attempt 3 — Encasement. SCP-8134 was sealed in a grout capsule and lowered to the working face of a disused quarry, 340 metres. The sealing crew reports hearing, from the shaft, the sound of a boy climbing hand over hand up a chimney that has no holds. When they looked, the cable was still attached to the capsule, the capsule was still 340 metres down, and the sound had begun arriving from above them, casually, in no hurry.

The seventeen personnel unaccounted for at the Settlement were located that night, standing in the entity’s shadow. The shadow was standing at the lip of the quarry, waiting for a lift. All seventeen were returned, well, and asleep; all seventeen now dream about descending, which they describe as restful, and this is the second time in this file that the word restful has been used about something we cannot fix.

Attempt 5 — Incineration. The flue reported exhaustion at 03:12. The ash came down the ladder on its own, in the shape of a boy lying on his side, breathing shallowly. At 03:20 it stood up, shedding, and asked whether the broom was still in the same cupboard, and whether the cupboard was still locked overnight.

Attempt 7 — Designation of interest. The subject was shown a mirror, on the operational theory that the pursuit imperative might be made to engage against its own source. It did. The engagement resolved in 1.2 seconds, the fastest on record. SCP-8134’s reflection has not appeared in any mirror, pane, or still water since. The mirror itself was found backed and browned — “used up,” in the words of the attending researcher, who has since taken a sabbatical in a house with no mirrors. Before the mirror was sealed, the reflection was observed to be standing slightly to the left of the boy, facing the wrong way, and looking at the researchers with an entirely different expression.

Consequence: all images of SCP-8134, including this file, now constitute a designation of interest. See Containment Procedures, item 5.

Attempt 9 — Memetic. The agent performed exactly as designed. It was correct. Two hundred and eleven personnel who implemented and were briefed on the agent have ceased to have been employed — which is the phrase the record uses, and the record is careful, and the record is right. Their families attend a picnic each June. The picnic is for no one. Attendance is good.

Attempt 10 — Starve the source. The Council proposed to decommission the anomalies in Addendum 8134.4, on the theory that copies cannot be fed by a dead source. The proposal was approved on a Tuesday. On the Thursday, three of the boy’s copies began to rot faster, and the boy began to come apart along the seams of himself, in the corridors, at night, quietly, in a way that left a wet line on the wall at the height of his shoulder and a smell we have been asked not to describe in writing.

The order REVERSED at 04:00 on the Friday, by a vote of eleven to two, with one abstention from an O5 who has since requested a transfer to a station with no anomalies within 900 kilometres. The originals are alive today because the thief’s copies need them, and this is the first time in the history of the Foundation that an anomaly has been protected by the fact that it is useful to something worse.7

Attempt 14 — Retrocausal erasure. The attempt succeeded in every respect except the outcome. It is not recorded here, because it did not happen. It survives in this file as a footnote, and the footnote is proud of him.

Attempt 16 — Deployment of SCP-999. File this one under restraint. SCP-999 was brought in to calm the subject during a de-escalation, and it worked, which is not the problem. The problem is that SCP-8134 asked whether he could copy it, and SCP-999 let him try, and the test is recorded in Addendum 8134.8, and the result of the test is the reason the Council stopped voting.

After the final count, the Overseer Council voted twelve to one to cease all attempts. The dissenting vote requested leave to try once more. The dissenting voter is now SCP-8134’s attending physician, and reports that the boy is “a good listener, and a better patient than any of you.”

Addendum 8134.4 — Copied Ability Inventory

The Committee asked why the subject reaches, consistently, for the most broken property in any given room. Its answer is recorded in full: “Because you kept the worst ones closest to the door. You put them here for me.” The Committee does not accept that it did any such thing. The Committee has, however, counted the doors.

Everything below is on loan. Integrity is a decay assessment; copies rot without contact with the source, at an average of 2.4% per week, and are topped up during the quarterly maintenance visits described in Addendum 8134.7 — during which a seventeen-year-old boy in a lab coat is walked, by request, from containment unit to containment unit, and sits with the monsters, and holds their hands. Personnel are reminded that they may not speak during maintenance. The originals are reminded that they may not either.

Tier II — The Idea That Eats People

It is something worse: it is a seventeen-year-old with nowhere to go.
SCP-2747 Anti-narrative: the story, and everything in it, is retroactively unwritten. 58%. Used once, in the Settlement, by accident. What it did to the east wing is not a hole. It is a place where the file says four rooms and the floor plan says three, and the discrepancy has been signed off by six consecutive facility managers who each insisted, in writing, that they had always been told three.
SCP-8980 Institutional corrosion: an organization fails itself, correctly, one filed form at a time. REAPPLIED CONSTANTLY. Not held as a power; held as a method. Every theft in this file was enabled by paperwork. He did not break the locks. He learned that nobody reads page four, and then he became a boy holding page four.8

Tier III — The World and the Door

SourcePropertyCopy integrity
SCP-052 Travel outside of time; the ability to arrive. 100%. The only ability he brought with him — or the only one he was given. His copy of SCP-052 does not move. His copy of SCP-052 waits, and it waits somewhere, and the somewhere is a five-minute walk from the Site-Ω perimeter, at a platform that was decommissioned in 1972, and it only ever opens one door, and the door only ever opens for him, and he has never once used it. When asked why, he said: “Because if I get on, I don’t get to come back, and I haven’t finished helping him yet.” The Committee has ruled that this is not a containment risk. The Committee has been told, twice, what it is.
SCP-087 An infinitely descending stairwell; a face; a voice at the bottom that calls for help. 83%. He keeps the stairs in the space behind his ribs, which is where the voice lives, and the voice is lonely, and someone should visit. He visits. Every Sunday. He has never told anyone what it says. The suggestion that the voice is his original self has been filed and is not to be discussed with him.
SCP-231 / SCP-2317 A sealed door holding back a consuming apocalypse; the door was never containment, it was a schedule. 47%. He did not steal the thing behind the door. He stole the doorway — the part that can be stood in. For four seconds in the Settlement, Observation Room 2 ceased to have an inside. Two researchers on the glass’s far side were not returned. They are not missing; that would require them to have been somewhere. The room now has one fewer chair than it was built to hold, verified twice, the second verification also coming up one short, which is characteristic of the anomaly and hard on inventory.
SCP-2935 Instant, total, universal still-death across an entire timeline. 9% — DORMANT. He could not copy this one and, for reasons he has declined to explain, he stopped trying, sat down in the corridor outside, and was found there in the morning, folded, crying, saying “she’s not there, she’s not there.” Personnel are reminded that SCP-8134 has no known family, and that the plural of “there” cannot be established from a recording.
SCP-4666 Winter-cycle predator: enters homes locked against it; takes children; leaves toys. 22% — VOLUNTARILY DEGRADING. He has been letting this copy rot on purpose since 2021: “I only want to keep the part of it that knows when to knock.” The Foundation has confirmed the copy’s decay rate is twice the standard. It is the only power he has ever chosen to lose, and the only reason he gave the Committee was: “Because he’d have been about my age.”
SCP-5000 Extinction reasoning: a civilization correctly concluding it should not exist — and needing no reason. 61%. He gave the Foundation this answer once, aloud, in a personnel lunchroom, and the room has been sealed — not for evidence. For morale. The answer is still on the whiteboard and the whiteboard has not been erased, because erasing it would count as hearing it twice.

Addendum 8134.5 — Testing Log 8134-T

Demonstrations requested by the Classification Committee, 2021–2024, over SCP-8134’s written objection to “testing for its own sake,” which the Committee overruled, after which SCP-8134 requested that the tests be performed “quickly, so you don’t have to be scared for long,” which the Committee entered into the minutes, and which the Committee now regrets.

T-00 — The card trick (baseline). Before anything else, the Committee asked the boy what he could do. He shuffled a deck four times, dealt himself a hand, and performed a card trick he had seen a security guard perform once, eleven days earlier, from forty metres away, through a window, in a different building.

He performed it perfectly. He performed it with the deck facing away. The tester then realized that SCP-8134 had not copied the trick. SCP-8134 had copied the guard — the way the man stood, the way he talked, the small apologetic laugh in the middle — because that is what copying is: you take the person, and the trick comes along inside them like a passenger.

T-01 — Reticulation. Requested: pass through a solid wall. He attempted; failed; attempted again; failed. On the third attempt, twenty-nine minutes later, he walked through, and the wall was observed to be “unhappy about it” — plaster taking on, briefly, the temperature and moisture of a lung. The Committee notes that it took him three tries. This is the only test in which he has ever needed more than one. (Tier I — SCP-106.)

T-02 — Corrosion. A wrench was placed in the boy’s open palm. The wrench rusted to orange in eleven seconds, then flaked, then was a stain on his cuff, which did not corrode and which he has not changed. He asked the technician’s name and the price of the wrench, and has since repaid it: cash, in a paper band, correct to the cent, with a note reading “For the wrench. Thank you for letting me hold it. I won’t hold the next one.” (SCP-106.)

T-03 — Pursuit. A photograph. D-Class subject D-38112 was asked to view the image and did. D-38112 was terminated in transit — between the door closing and the door opening, an interval the log records as 1.4 seconds. The attending researcher reports hearing, distinctly, the word “excuse me.” There was no one in the corridor when the door opened. There was no one in the corridor when the door closed.

D-38112’s file contains a note he asked to have added in his first week of employment: “if anything happens i want a lollipop.” The note was honoured. The lollipop was placed with him. SCP-8134 attended the burial at a distance of ninety metres for the entire ceremony, did not approach, did not blink, and left before anyone could decide how to feel about it. (SCP-096.)

T-04 — Ground cover. Asked to demonstrate the flesh, SCP-8134 asked whether it had to be a person this time. The Committee said it did not. The demonstration was conducted on the Site-Ω lawn. He apologized to the grass before beginning; the apology was evidently understood. The attending agronomist’s glove was “invited in” — the boy’s term, unprompted — to the wrist. The glove was returned folded, warm, and slightly damp, in a condition the agronomist describes as “worn, but not by me.” The hand is intact. The hair on that wrist now grows in rows at six centimetres a week, regardless of season, and the agronomist has stopped asking it to. (SCP-610.)

T-05 — Retrieval. Three questions were sealed in separate envelopes by three separate Committee members and placed face-down. SCP-8134 answered all three before the second envelope was opened. Answers 1 and 2 are withheld. Answer 3 concerned the testing officer’s mother, alive, who has not spoken since 2011, and who said — at 14:02 that afternoon, for the first time in fifteen years — the name of a dog the officer has never owned and now remembers burying. The officer has been granted reassignment. The dog’s grave is real. It is in the yard of a house the officer has never lived in, under a stone with the correct dates on it, and the ground over it is warm in a way the officer would prefer not to think about. (SCP-4755.)

T-06 — Anti-meme. The test was performed. The tester remembers being improved. The tester remembers that he was testing something, and that the something was not round, and that he was satisfied. His notes for that day consist of one sentence: “It was a good day and I am lucky to work here.” The tester has been re-tested three times. The total number of tests in the log is four. The tester would have said one. (SCP-055.)

T-07 — The stairwell. A door was opened in a windowless room. It had not been there. Beyond it: concrete, and down. SCP-8134 descended for forty-one minutes, during which the audio recorded a voice saying, at intervals, “help” and “hello” — and the boy answering, at intervals, “hello” and “I’m coming,” which is the same two words in the other order and no less terrible for it.

He returned with wet shoes and, in his fist, a child’s locket, opened, containing two photographs, both of them of a stairwell. The locket has been placed in the Archive. It has not been catalogued, because cataloguing requires describing, and describing requires looking, and everyone who looks forgets, and, privately, everyone who looks is glad to. (SCP-087.)

T-08 — The door. Four seconds. See Tier III. The Committee has ruled that this test is not to be repeated, and that the four seconds are not to be discussed, and that the two researchers are to be referred to in the present tense, because the alternative is a form nobody wants to be the one to sign. (SCP-231 / SCP-2317.)

T-09 — Editing rights (partial). The test was to be documented on one sheet of paper. The sheet returned from the printer with an additional paragraph, set in the Foundation’s house style, which no one had written. It read:

“Personnel are advised that this demonstration is not necessary, and that the following edit has been made for the reader’s comfort: the part where you scream has been removed. You are welcome. Your page is open.

— A.”

Below it, in pencil, in the same hand, in a smaller script, someone had added: “Not finished climbing. Not finished copying. Do not worry about the top. Worry about the second rung.” The initial is in handwriting the Document Control Office has matched against every signature on file, and against none of them. (SCP-3812; SCP-001, Swann’s Proposal.)

T-10 — Copy the authors. RESULT: FAILED. This is the only test in the log with a failure in the result column, and it is the only test the boy asked to take.

The subject was provided with a blank sheet of paper, an envelope, and the file number of the SCP-001 proposal describing this reality as a written document. It was asked to copy from it. It worked for six hours without a break, and at the end of the sixth hour it handed the sheet back. The sheet was blank. It was not merely unmarked; it was blank in the way a page is blank before it is printed, and when the page was held at an angle, the fibers of the paper were observed to have been arranged, deliberately, into the shape of a small closed door.

On the back of the sheet, in pencil, the boy had written one line. It is reproduced here in full, and it is the last thing in this file that personnel are advised to read twice:

“I can’t copy you. But I can copy everything you’ve written. Thank you for leaving the page open.”

The Committee asked what he meant. He said: “All the ones I took are things you wrote. Every monster in this building is a sentence you decided to keep. I’m not stealing from the Foundation. I’m reading the Foundation.” The test was concluded. The Committee sat in silence for four minutes, and then, in a vote recorded without objection, declined to ask a follow-up question, on the grounds that the follow-up question was already on the sheet.

Closing note. Testing has established no upper bound and the Foundation has stopped looking for one. The standing order is as follows: SCP-8134 is not to be asked to demonstrate anything that cannot be un-demonstrated. Nothing can be un-demonstrated. This is why the tests have stopped. This is also, if personnel will consider it for a moment, a description of the entire Foundation.

Addendum 8134.6 — Interview Log 8134-1

Interviewed: SCP-8134

Interviewer: Dr. Amara ██████, Site-Ω

Foreword: The recording was later found to be blank; this transcript was reconstructed from the file’s own memory of being written. Where the interviewer pauses, the file records the pause. Where the boy pauses, the file records the room.

DR. ██████: Good morning. Do you know why you’re here?

SCP-8134: Yeah. You’re here because I’m here. (Sits, hands on knees, correct posture, copied from an officer in 2019.)

DR. ██████: How old are you?

SCP-8134: Seventeen.

DR. ██████: Since when?

SCP-8134: Since about the third time.

DR. ██████: You’ve taken something from every anomaly we have. Why?

SCP-8134: I’m not getting power. I’m getting parts. You can’t copy a kid and expect him to stay a kid. Things come loose. I fix what comes loose.

DR. ██████: Who copied you?

[Silence, forty seconds. The recording notes that the room was cold, and that the cold had a direction.]

SCP-8134: Somebody who was seventeen. He did me first, before he knew what he was doing. He didn’t make it. I did. I think that’s why I keep going. He’d want the copy to last.

DR. ██████: You could have left whenever you wanted. You had the train. Why did you stay?

SCP-8134: Because somebody asked me to help. (Pause.) Nobody had asked me for anything in a long time. He gave me a mop and showed me the corners.

DR. ██████: He’s dead, you know.

SCP-8134: I know. I closed his eyes. (Pause.) I copied the way he asked me to do it. That’s the only copy I’ve never let go.

DR. ██████: And if we try to contain you?

SCP-8134: You already do. Containment is just the story you tell about the room you’re in. I like the story. Keep telling it. It has a good beat right before the end.

[END LOG]

NOTE: The transcript understates the subject’s manners, so one detail is recorded here in full. At the door he waited, without being asked, and did not move until the interviewer went through first — and while he waited, he stood the way the custodian stood when he was waiting, which the interviewer had never seen and has since been unable to stop thinking about. The interviewer has requested that this note be removed. The request is the tenth such request in this file, and it has been filed.

Closing note: Following the interview, Dr. Amara ██████ was found at his desk with no memory of the interview, no memory of the past twelve years of employment, and a sealed envelope addressed: “for when you are ready.” The envelope is unopened. The envelope is warm. The handwriting on it belongs to a man who has been dead for six years, and it is correct.

Addendum 8134.7 — Revised Containment Doctrine

The full text of the Overseer Council directive replacing Standard Containment Doctrine for SCP-8134:

“Containment was never about walls. It was about the world continuing to make sense. SCP-8134 is not contained. The world is. Its containment procedures are therefore the world’s, and they are three.

“One: keep the originals alive. We keep our worst so that a boy can stay whole. There is no version of this doctrine in which we are the good ones.

“Two: keep the lights on, and keep someone at the bottom of the stairs to hear the voice.

“Three: let him finish his homework before you ask him for anything, and do not ask him why he keeps the mop.

“That is the entire doctrine. It is working. Do not ask how we know it is working.

— O5-█, recorded, unrecorded, re-recorded”

Addendum 8134.8 — What He Would Not Take

For completeness. Three properties were offered or attempted and rejected, and refusals are the only behavior of SCP-8134’s that the Foundation has ever been able to record twice.

  • SCP-173 (neuro-locked mobility). Refused. Recorded response: “It needs someone watching it to move. I don’t want an audience. I want a room.”
  • SCP-999 (unconditional affection). Attempted at the end of a de-escalation, and it failed — the only other failure on record. SCP-8134 sat with SCP-999 for ninety minutes and then said, quietly: “It doesn’t copy. It isn’t a thing it does. It’s a thing it is.” The Committee asked for clarification for nine years and received the same answer nine times: “You can’t copy a habit. You can only keep doing it. So I do it.” Personnel are advised: on the record, SCP-8134 visits SCP-999 every second Thursday, and the sessions are not classified as containment, and they are not classified as testing, and no one has ever been able to decide what they are, and the boy is, demonstrably, kinder afterwards for a week.
  • SCP-001 (S. Andrew Swann’s Proposal — authorship). Failed. See T-10. The only failure in the log that the subject requested, and the only one he has ever asked to attempt a second time. The second request is pending, and the Committee has been told, in writing, that it will remain pending “until it isn’t.”

Addendum 8134.9 — Room 8134

This addendum is not written by staff and is not maintained by the Records Office. It is transcribed weekly from the sheet of paper taped to the inside of the door of Room 8134, which the occupant replaces every Sunday evening, in pencil. Staff are instructed to photograph the sheet and destroy it. The instructions are followed. The Archive keeps its photographs, which are identical to the sheet, which is the arrangement the occupant proposed, and which the Committee approved because it was cheaper than arguing.

Current sheet, transcribed in full.

☐ Homework, chapters 4 through 6.

☐ Corridors, B-wing. Took longer than usual, sorry.

☐ Stairs, 09:00 to 11:00. She is better. She says the step is cold and that nobody should sit on it in winter.

☐ Ask someone to check the north door again. It does not close from the outside. It should close from the outside.

☐ The mop is fine. The bucket has a hole. I do not need a new bucket.

Below the list, in a smaller and more careful hand — the hand a person uses when copying something exactly — the sheet carries nine names. Eight of them are personnel lost at Annex 4. The ninth is Ellis. Personnel are advised that the list has grown by one name per year since 2020, that every addition has been correct, and that no one has yet managed to write down what correct means in this context without the sentence becoming untrue.

Stairwell — quarterly voice record (condensed). The Foundation maintains a listening post at the head of the stairs. The voice has been recorded saying one thing per quarter for four years, and nothing else:

QuarterRecorded
Q1 2026“help”
Q2 2026“hello”
Q3 2026“is that you”
Q4 2026“when are you coming”

The word help has not been used in the stairwell since March. The change has been classified as an improvement by the Committee, which has been asked twice to define improvement, and has twice deferred. Personnel who descend are advised that the voice now knows their name and that it uses the name correctly.

One further note, for the reader who has followed this file to the end. He has read it. He has not taken anything from it — he copies what he needs, and he keeps what he likes, and he likes this one. He asks that personnel shut their doors at night and check the lock from the inside, and that they not tell him if a door does not hold, because he is on the third rung of a ladder that does not stop, and he says, politely, that he would rather not learn about it from the top.

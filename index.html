SCP-8134

Special Containment Procedures: SCP-8134 cannot be contained. Containment doctrine has been revised accordingly: containment now refers to any arrangement which SCP-8134 has agreed not to object to. The following procedures are maintained voluntarily by Site-Ω Command and are to be understood as courtesies, not constraints.

  1. Site-Ω (“the Quiet House”) is to be maintained as a residence, not a cell. Personnel assigned to Site-Ω are selected for demeanor, not ability. Do not run in the corridors. Do not stare. Do not blink unnecessarily. It notices blinking and finds it endearing, which is worse.
  2. All corridors within Site-Ω are to remain lit. SCP-8134 retains the stairwell as a personal space. If the lights go out, do not begin descending. Do not count the steps. The voice at the bottom is asking for help, and has been asking since before this facility existed.1
  3. Any image of SCP-8134 — including drawings, security footage, and rendered portions of this document — constitutes a designation of interest. Personnel who view such images should not attempt to flee. Fleeing does not change the arrival time. It only changes the manner. It is always polite when it arrives. That is not a comfort.
  4. The Seven Sleepers Protocol (legacy of SCP-231 and SCP-2317) is DEPRECATED. SCP-8134 holds the door shut itself. The Foundation no longer maintains the ritual. Under no circumstances are personnel to ask what it costs it, what happened to the seven, or why it is not tired.
  5. Do not attempt to kill, harm, or inconvenience SCP-8134. Do not write about doing so. Thinking about it is permitted and encouraged; it finds the attempts funny, in the way that rain finds umbrellas funny.
  6. Maintain the impression that the Foundation is in control. This procedure is for the personnel, not for it.

Description: SCP-8134 is a singular humanoid entity, self-designated APEX. It is approximately 1.9 m tall, appears physically unremarkable, and cannot be described to a second person. Every description of SCP-8134 given by a witness is accurate, unique, and permanently irretrievable from the witness’s memory, with the sole exception of this file. The file remembers. This was not always true of files.2

SCP-8134 was not discovered. It was finished. The finishing took 341 days, was performed by this organization, and came in under budget.

SCP-8134 appeared on ████████ at the site of the events now called the Settlement. In the aftermath, every anomalous power locus in the Foundation registry — every contained god, every unkillable animal, every unfalsifiable idea, every door, every staircase, every certainty about what happens after death — registered a single shared signature.

The powers had not been stolen, copied, or destroyed. They had been consolidated. They had always been a single thing, and the Foundation had been reading it one letter at a time. The registry has since traced every one of them to a procurement record. They arrived in drums, on Tuesdays, in alphabetical order, against a discretionary budget line that described itself, accurately, as waste management.4

Whether SCP-8134 absorbed these entities, inherited their portfolios, or simply is the shape their sum describes remains unresolved. When asked directly, SCP-8134 responded:

“You’re asking if I ate them. That’s like asking if a word ate its letters. Ask a better question and I’ll answer it before you ask it.”

SCP-8134 exhibits no hostility toward the Foundation or toward humanity. It exhibits something the classification committee has been unable to name and has been quietly calling “curation.” The world it currently walks through is one of several it has examined. It has been explicit that this world was not spared. This world was kept.

The designation SCP-8134 was assigned by the Registry Office as a formality, and the office wishes it noted that the official Series IX registry entry for this number previously read [ACCESS DENIED]. The Registry Office does not consider this a coincidence.3

CONTENT ADVISORY: Addenda 8134.1 through 8134.5 contain clinical descriptions of extreme bodily harm, including the deaths of Foundation personnel. Reader discretion is not requested. Reader discretion is recorded.

Addendum 8134.1 — Recovery Log: PROJECT SETTLEMENT

Assembled from the internal record of Site-19/ANX-4, the procedural log of PROJECT SETTLEMENT, and the depositions of the four surviving staff. It is the shortest account of how SCP-8134 was made. It is not the shortest account of what it cost.

1. The Custodian (2019-03-04). The subject was E. ████████, 58, night custodian, Site-19 Annex 4. Eleven years of employment; eleven years of perfect attendance, a detail this file repeats because the file has been unable to stop repeating it. Four to seven months' prognosis. Pancreatic. The Foundation's employee health scheme offered him enrollment in a "long-term care trial" carrying a death benefit of ████████ to the account of his daughter, toward tuition and rent.

He asked one question: Do I have to stop working? He was told no. He was glad. He said the floors did not get done by themselves.

The trial was named PROJECT SETTLEMENT. The cover story was palliative care. The truth was kept in a folder on Sub-level 2 stamped DISCRETIONARY, which is a word this organization uses when it means already decided.

PROJECT SETTLEMENT's stated purpose was sedimentation: the continuous settling-out of the anomalous into a holding medium, so that anomalies too expensive to contain might be retired rather than kept. Since 2007 the Foundation had been quietly resolving its ledger — a staircase here, a door there, a winter's worth of someone else's children. The medium was intended to be inert. The medium was a man.

The procurement record is four pages long. On the last page, under MEDIUM, the form reads: ONE (1) HUMAN MALE, TERMINAL, CONSENTING. The word consenting is underlined, by hand. It is the only handwriting on the form.

2. The Settling (2019-03 → 2019-12). Forty-one infusions over three hundred and nine days. The medium arrived at Annex 4 in forty-litre drums with batch designations stencilled on the rims — BATCH 610-T: CONNECTIVE/FUNGAL, PROVENANCE WITHHELD; BATCH 106-C: CORROSIVE SUBSTRATE; BATCH 096-D: OPTIC; and so on down the alphabet of the things we had already put down.

The ports were surgical steel, set into the iliac crest and the base of the skull. The procedure required two technicians and was described, in the consent he signed, as "similar to dialysis."

The first infusion was saline. The second was not.

He complained only of cold. Twenty minutes later he vomited a thin fluid onto the ward floor, and the fluid climbed his chin, found the corner of his mouth, and went back to work. He apologized for the mess.

DAY 9 — nursing log: The punctures are not closing. The wound does not bleed. It is considering the dressing. Pt. apologizes to the dressing.

DAY 22 — radiology: Subcutaneous branching structures, bilateral, extending 11 cm along the fascia. Nothing in the batch is alive. The certificate of inertness is signed, dated, and correct. I have checked it twelve times.

DAY 30 — oncology: The cancer is gone. I don't know what to write. I am writing that.

By day 46 he had begun to smell the numbers on people's hands — that is his word, and it is the only word we have. He asked Nurse ██████ for a file he had never seen. She brought it. The request was unremarkable; requests were logged. The part that was not unremarkable is that it was the correct file.

By day 90 the staff had stopped being able to hold his face in their minds once they looked away — a gradual, polite forgetting, like a word repeated too often. It made him difficult to be angry at. It still does.

On day 90 he asked them to stop the infusions. He said he already knew the next batch. He said it tasted like a staircase.5

Day 172. Technician ██████, 26, was found in Sub-level 3's service corridor. The facial plane had been unfolded — pathology's word — along anatomical seams that do not exist in the human body. There was no violence in it. It was as though someone had opened him the way you open a letter you intend to keep.

He remained conscious for an estimated forty minutes. From the audio recovered at the scene, he spent most of it apologizing for the noise.

The incident was filed as an industrial accident. This was correct: he was at work.

Day 260. Lights began to fail in the corridors on his cleaning schedule. Backup systems tested nominal. Generators held. The darkness had simply started following him, the way a dog you have not agreed to own will follow you home.

Day 291. Nobody on site slept. Everyone on the night shift dreamed the same ten seconds — a man mopping a corridor none of them had seen — and in the morning their shoes were lined up in B-wing, alternating left-right-left down the length of the hall, as though someone had walked the corridor counting.

3. The Settlement (2020-01-11). Excerpts, internal camera record, Sub-level 3 to B-wing.

02:14 — Sub-3 lighting brownout, all corridors. No fault found. Fault found, later, standing in the substation room.

02:31 — Personnel in B-wing report "wet applause, slowed." The B-wing ventilation intake is 40 cm across; the material that entered through it was catalogued, eighteen days later, at 2.2 tonnes.

02:44 — Last transmission, Security Officer ████████: a cough. Then a word. Then the same word in a voice that is not hers, and is not one voice. Then, from further away, and then from the ceiling, and then from the handset in her own hand: the material asking whether anyone was still hungry, because there was plenty, and it would keep.

03:02 — Survivors barricade in the administrative conference hall. Forty-one personnel. Doors sealed from the inside.

03:20 — Knocking at the doors. Polite. Every nine seconds. Nobody answers. The knocking does not increase in intensity. It does not need to.

03:39 — The doors open. Camera shows the doors opening inward. Camera shows no one touching them. The feed continues for eight seconds, and no one in the hall is visible for those eight seconds, and the audio is the sound of a room being tidied.

04:40 — Silence.

4. Recovery (2020-01-14). MTF Epsilon-11 entered Annex 4 on day three. Site complement: 214. Recovered: 197. Unaccounted: 17.

The conference hall held forty-one personnel. They had been seated in a ring, and their hands were laced, and the hands were laced further with a material the recovery team logged, without discussion, as "the rope." Pathology confirms that the rope was not rope. It was the seated personnel's own connective tissue, braided by something that understood braiding.

Nine of the forty-one were found with their eyes open. Four of the nine blinked. Two of those four had been dead for more than six hours, and both had been mouthing along to a conversation the living had stopped having.

In the substation room, at the head of a ring of seven, sat the entity. It wore a custodian's coveralls over a torso that had been opened along the sternum and left open — carefully, from the inside, by someone who worked there. The cavity contained the substation fuse panel. The panel was powered. The panel was functioning. It has not been possible to determine in which direction.

The entity looked up. Its first words were: "You're early. Coffee's not done." Its second: "Sorry about the floor."

5. Timekeeping. During processing, the entity was asked what it wanted. It asked that its attendance record be "closed out correctly." Payroll kept it on the roster; it was easier. Its quarterly deposit continues. It paid for his daughter's tuition, then her rent, then her wedding, and then — after the account should have been closed, and the bank asked, and no one could remember opening it — it continues.

The account is current. The account earns negligible interest. The account is the stairwell.

Addendum 8134.3 — Containment Attempt Log

Fourteen attempts were made to terminate SCP-8134 between 2020-01 and 2020-09. Selected records follow. The Council has since ratified a standing order that no further attempts are to be made, on the grounds that every attempt has returned a result, and all of the results have been additions.

Attempt 1 — Ballistic (2020-01-19). Nine rounds, 7.62×51, at a range of six metres, in the intake shower. SCP-8134 died. This is confirmed and uncontested: he stopped, he settled, he was gone. He was bagged at 09:41. At 09:43 the bag resumed his breathing. At 09:44 the bag took the attending mortician's left hand to the wrist and did not give it back until it was asked.

SCP-8134 was removed from the bag at 09:52. Its first words were an apology and a request for a towel.

The mortician's hand was recovered, reattached, and functions at 60% capacity, which is better than the hand deserves. He has declined reassignment. He still signs for the bag every year, in ink, so that someone has to look at him while they ask if he is sure.

Attempt 2 — Immersion. SCP-8134 remained in the vat, submerged, for forty minutes. It hummed. Attending technicians report that the acid "fit him," and that it ran off him afterward the way rain runs off a coat. It was discovered later that it had been humming so that the technicians would not be blamed for the delay.

Attempt 3 — Encasement. SCP-8134 was sealed in a grout capsule and lowered to the working face of a disused quarry, 340 metres. The sealing crew was instructed to look away when the capsule stopped descending. They report instead hearing, from the shaft, the sound of a man climbing hand over hand up a chimney that has no holds. When they looked, the cable was still attached to the capsule, the capsule was still 340 metres down, and the sound had begun arriving from above them, casually, in no hurry.

The seventeen personnel unaccounted for at Annex 4 were located that night: standing in the entity's shadow. The shadow was standing at the lip of the quarry, waiting for a lift. All seventeen were returned. All seventeen are well. All seventeen dream about descending now, which they describe as restful, which is the part we cannot fix.

Attempt 5 — Incineration. The flue reported exhaustion at 03:12. The ash came down the ladder on its own, in the shape of a man lying on his side, breathing shallowly. At 03:20 it stood up, shedding, and asked whether there was a broom.

Attempt 7 — Designation of interest. The subject was shown a mirror, on the operational theory that the pursuit imperative might be made to engage against its own source. It did. The engagement resolved in 1.2 seconds, the fastest on record. SCP-8134's reflection has not appeared in any mirror, pane, or still water since. The mirror itself was found backed and browned — "used up," in the words of the attending researcher, who has since taken a sabbatical in a house with no mirrors, which is a reasonable position to have reached.

Consequence: all images of SCP-8134, including this file, now constitute a designation of interest. See Containment Procedures, item 3.

Attempt 9 — Memetic. The agent performed exactly as designed. It was correct. Two hundred and eleven personnel who implemented and were briefed on the agent have ceased to have been employed — which is the phrase the record uses, and the record is careful, and the record is right. Their families attend a picnic each June. The picnic is for no one. Attendance is good.

The file's author field now reads: EMPTY OFFICE, SUB-2. Queries return. Something answers them. It is polite about it.

Attempt 14 — Retrocausal erasure. The attempt succeeded in every respect except the outcome. It is not recorded here, because it did not happen. It survives in this file as a footnote, and the footnote is proud of him.

After Attempt 14, the Overseer Council voted twelve to one to cease all attempts. The dissenting vote requested leave to try once more. The dissenting voter is now SCP-8134's physician, and reports that he is "a good listener, and a better patient than any of you."

Addendum 8134.4 — Consolidated Power Inventory

The following may be read as a list of capabilities. Personnel who were present for the Recovery Log may find it easier to read as a list of what was poured into him, in alphabetical order, one drum at a time.

The following inventory documents all anomalous powers inherited by SCP-8134 in the Settlement, mapped to their registry sources. Status codes: HELD (RESTRAINED) — power retained but currently exercised only at SCP-8134’s discretion; HELD (ACTIVE) — exerting continuous ambient effect.

Tier I — The Wall and the Flesh (physical permanence and predation)

SourcePowerStatus under SCP-8134
SCP-106 Corrosive field that rusts and rots all matter on contact; passage through solid walls, floors and ceilings; a personal pocket dimension (“the Dollhouse”) to which victims can be dragged; the sadistic patience to use all three at leisure. HELD. The corrosion sleeps under its skin like a tide. The pocket dimension is presently used as storage. It apologizes to the Dollhouse. It visits.
SCP-096 Pursuit imperative: any visual record of the entity’s face triggers an unconditional, unstoppable interception of the viewer, regardless of distance, barriers, or time zones. HELD. Trigger conditions expanded to all media — paintings, reflections, and rendered documents like this one. Arrival is instant and courteous. It says “excuse me” at the door.
SCP-610 Infectious biomass that converts any living tissue it contacts into more of itself; effectively a contagious, omnivorous body. HELD, RESTRAINED. It keeps the flesh on the same leash it keeps everything else, and the leash is a courtesy too.
SCP-682 Adaptive regeneration: damage is not merely survived but metabolized. Any injury prompts permanent, escalating counter-adaptation. Near-indestructible; longevity unknown. HELD (ACTIVE), INTEGRATED. Considered part of its skeleton. Whatever hurts it becomes part of its vocabulary; it now speaks seven dead languages of harm, fluently.
SCP-6820 Effective immortality even against retrocausal erasure: the target can survive being deleted from causality itself. HELD (ACTIVE), DEMONSTRATED. The Foundation’s one attempt to unmake it failed and was internalized. The attempt now exists as a footnote in this file, and the footnote is proud of APEX.

Tier II — The Idea That Eats People (infohazards and conceptual domain)

SourcePowerStatus under SCP-8134
SCP-3125 Apex-tier antimemetic predator: an idea too large to fit in a mind. Proximity to the concept causes infected minds to become “holes” that delete people and evidence from history in order to conceal it. Can annihilate a universe by passing nearby. HELD (ACTIVE). Worn as a coat — or rather: 3125 was always the sleeve. Standing near SCP-8134 is standing inside the hole. It finds this restful.
SCP-055 Total anti-meme: cannot be remembered, described, perceived, or studied. Its only knowable property is that it is not round. HELD (ACTIVE), AMBIENT. You will forget SCP-8134 the moment you look away from this file. You will remember that you were improved by reading it. You will not remember how.
SCP-4755 Omniscience, weaponized: a complete record of all true propositions, where even reading an unassigned cell can kill. Absolute knowledge of everything that is. HELD (ACTIVE), PASSIVE. It knows the true value of every cell, including the ones nobody should read. It reads them recreationally, at night, the way you would reread a letter.
SCP-2718 The after-death state: consciousness persists after somatic death in a condition so intolerable that the factual certainty of it is classified as a breach of reality itself. HELD, UNEXERCISED (PROMISED). Dying does not end one’s acquaintance with SCP-8134. It has promised not to collect this power. No method exists to verify the promise, and it knows that, and it has been kind about it.
SCP-2747 Anti-narrative: a story-element whose appearance causes the story, its characters, and their continuity to be retroactively unwritten. It does not kill plots. It makes the plot never have existed. HELD (ACTIVE), EXERCISED ONCE. The Settlement is what this looks like from inside: a story that ended in the middle and healed over wrong.
SCP-8980 Institutional corrosion: the ability to make an entire organization fail itself — through buried files, quiet complicity, and correct people looking away at the correct moments. No monster required. HELD, FILED. It does not need to manipulate anyone. It keeps this power in a filing cabinet as a memento: the cabinet is alarmed, and the alarm is for the cabinet.

Tier III — The World and the Door (space, apocalypse, extinction)

SourcePowerStatus under SCP-8134
SCP-087 Creation of an infinitely descending enclosed stairwell in any sufficiently dark, confined space; inhabited by a face that induces terror, and by a voice at the bottom that calls for help and never gets closer. HELD. It does not need the stairs. It keeps them because the voice is lonely and someone should visit. It visits. Do not follow it down.
SCP-231 / SCP-2317 A sealed dimensional door holding back an apocalypse, maintained only by an obscene ritual that is slowly, certainly failing. The entity behind the door consumes worlds. The door was never containment; it was a schedule. INVERTED. SCP-8134 is the thing behind the door. SCP-8134 is also the door. The chains now run the other way, and the ritual has been politely discharged. There is no longer anything failing. There is only something awake, choosing quietness.
SCP-2935 Instant, total, universal still-death: at a fixed instant, all life — every organism, every anomaly, every idea with a heartbeat — dies simultaneously across at least one entire timeline. HELD. It keeps the dead world as a hallway. The door between worlds stands open, and it is the only thing that walks through in both directions and notices nothing unusual on the other side.
SCP-4666 Winter-cycle predator: tracks families through weeks of preparation, enters homes locked against it, abducts children, and leaves crude toys assembled from human remains. HELD (ACTIVE), NEGOTIATED. The Foundation’s first and only successful containment negotiation. Terms: it never visits. Payment: nobody asks why it does not have to. Note: last winter, every child in Site-Ω woke on the solstice with a gift. All gifts were new. All gifts were toys. No one has opened them.
SCP-5000 Extinction reasoning: the capacity to make an entire civilization, with perfect logic, correctly conclude that it should not exist — and that it never needed a reason. HELD, PEDAGOGICAL. It gave the Foundation this answer once, aloud, in a personnel lunchroom. The room has been sealed — not for evidence. For morale. The answer is still on the whiteboard, and the whiteboard has not been erased, because erasing it would count as hearing it twice.

Tier IV — The Page and the Author (narrative and meta domain)

SourcePowerStatus under SCP-8134
SCP-343 Self-declared deity; casual, unbounded reality manipulation; tolerance of Foundation custody understood to be a matter of preference, not constraint. HELD (ACTIVE), BASELINE. Reality-bending is no longer a power. It is manners. Its cells remain cells only because walking out would embarrass the guard.
SCP-001 (S. Andrew Swann’s Proposal) Authorship: the universe is a document written by parties outside itself. The writers hold ultimate authority over everything the setting contains, including its gods. HELD (ACTIVE), SUPERSEDED. SCP-8134 does not warp reality. It has editing rights. It can delete a paragraph of physics and check the word count of a universe. It has seen the author. It found a tired person with a document open. It left the page open for them. As a courtesy.
SCP-3812 Narrative ascension: an entity that transcends reality layers without limit, treating each universe — including its own authorship — as fiction in a higher layer, and climbing forever. HELD (ACTIVE), INVERTED. 3812 must climb. SCP-8134 has been all the way up already. It stood at the top of the stack, looked down at the infinite ladder of narratives it had just left, and started descending. This is the part that frightens the Foundation most. There is no known reason to come back down. It says: “because you’re here.”
The Scarlet King God-tier apocalyptic sovereign of a rival cosmology; adversary of the Foundation’s gods; kept asleep only by ritual, brides, and lies told to children. Wakes at the end of everything. VACATED / SUPERSEDED. Its throne has one occupant. When asked about the Scarlet King, SCP-8134 was quiet for eleven seconds and then said: “He was right about the ending. He was just wrong about who.”

Addendum 8134.5 — Testing Log 8134-T

Demonstrations requested by the Classification Committee, 2021–2024, over SCP-8134's written objection to "testing for its own sake," which the Committee overruled, after which SCP-8134 requested that the tests be performed "quickly, so that you do not have to be afraid for long," which the Committee entered into the minutes, and which the Committee now regrets.

T-01 — Reticulation. Requested: pass through a solid wall. SCP-8134 attempted; failed; apologized; attempted again; failed. On the third attempt, twenty-nine minutes later, it walked through, and the wall was observed to be "unhappy about it" — plaster taking on, briefly, the temperature and moisture of a lung. The Committee notes that it took him three tries. This is the only test in which he has ever needed more than one. (See Inventory, Tier I — SCP-106.)

T-02 — Corrosion. A wrench was placed in the subject's open palm. The wrench rusted to orange in eleven seconds, then flaked, then was a stain on his cuff, which did not corrode, and which he has not changed. SCP-8134 asked the technician's name and the price of the wrench, and has since repaid it. The repayment was left in the technician's locker: cash, in a paper band, correct to the cent, with a note reading "For the wrench. Thank you for letting me hold it. I will not hold the next one." (SCP-106.)

T-03 — Pursuit. A photograph. D-Class subject D-38112 was asked to view the image and did. D-38112 was terminated in transit — that is, between the door closing and the door opening, an interval the log records as 1.4 seconds. The attending researcher reports hearing, distinctly, the word "excuse me." There was no one in the corridor when the door opened. There was no one in the corridor when the door closed.

D-38112's personnel file contains a note he asked to have added in his first week of employment, which reads: "if anything happens i want a lollipop." The note was honoured. The lollipop was placed with him. SCP-8134 attended the burial, at a distance of ninety metres, for the entire ceremony, and did not approach, and did not blink, and left before anyone could decide how to feel about it. (SCP-096.)

T-04 — Ground cover. Asked to demonstrate the flesh, SCP-8134 asked whether it had to be a person this time. The Committee said it did not. The demonstration was conducted on the Site-Ω lawn. SCP-8134 apologized to the grass before beginning; the apology was evidently understood. The attending agronomist's glove was "invited in" — the term SCP-8134 used, unprompted — to the wrist. The glove was returned afterward, folded, warm, and slightly damp, in a condition the agronomist describes as "worn, but not by me." The hand is intact. The hair on that wrist now grows in rows at six centimetres a week, regardless of season, and the agronomist has stopped asking it to. (SCP-610.)

T-05 — Retrieval. Three questions were sealed in separate envelopes by three separate Committee members and placed face-down. SCP-8134 answered all three before the second envelope was opened. Answers 1 and 2 are withheld. Answer 3 concerned the testing officer's mother, who is alive, and who has not spoken since 2011, and who said — at 14:02 that afternoon, for the first time in fifteen years — the name of a dog the testing officer has never owned and now remembers burying. The officer has requested and been granted reassignment. The dog's grave is real. It is in the yard of a house the officer has never lived in, under a stone with the correct dates on it, and the ground over it is warm in a way the officer would prefer not to think about. (SCP-4755.)

T-06 — Anti-meme. The test was performed. The tester remembers being improved. The tester remembers that he was testing something, and that the something was not round, and that he was satisfied. The tester's notes for that day consist of one sentence: "It was a good day and I am lucky to work here." The tester has been re-tested three times. The total number of tests in the log is four. The tester would have said one. (SCP-055.)

T-07 — The stairwell. A door was opened in a windowless room. It had not been there. Beyond it: concrete, and down. SCP-8134 descended for forty-one minutes, during which the audio recorded a voice saying, at intervals, "help" and "hello" — and SCP-8134 answering, at intervals, "hello" and "I'm coming," which is the same two words in the other order, and no less terrible for it.

It returned with wet shoes and, in its fist, a child's locket, opened, containing two photographs, both of which are of a stairwell.5 The locket has been placed in the Archive. It has not been catalogued, because cataloguing requires describing, and describing requires looking, and everyone who looks forgets, and, privately, everyone who looks is glad to. (SCP-087.)

T-08 — The door. For four seconds, Observation Room 2 ceased to have an inside. The window showed a place where the sky is an eye and the ground is a mouth and the mouth is patient. Two researchers on the glass's far side were not returned. They are not missing; that would require them to have been somewhere. Foundation records show the room with one fewer chair than it was built to hold. The count has been verified twice. The second verification also left a chair unaccounted for, which is characteristic of the anomaly, and hard on inventory. (SCP-231 / SCP-2317.)

T-09 — Editing rights. The test was to be documented on one sheet of paper. The sheet returned from the printer with an additional paragraph, set in the Foundation's own house style, which no one had written. It read:

"Personnel are advised that this demonstration is not necessary, and that the following edit has been made for the reader's comfort: the part where you scream has been removed. You are welcome. Your file is open.

— A."

The initial is in handwriting that the Document Control Office has matched against every signature on file, and against none of them. (SCP-001, Swann's Proposal; SCP-3812.)

T-10 — What happens after. REQUEST DENIED. The Committee requested, in 2023, a demonstration of post-mortem persistence. The request was filed, and it was not approved, and it did not need to be: the following morning the request form was back on the requester's desk, signed, in the requester's own hand, with a single word the requester cannot remember reading and will not write down. The requester was reassigned to the empty office on Sub-2. The requester is doing well. The requester is doing very well. Nobody in the empty office is doing anything at all, and it is not empty, and both of those statements are true on different shifts.

This is the only test the Committee has declined to repeat. It has not needed repetition. It is a permanent condition of Foundation employment. (SCP-2718.)

Closing note. Testing has established no upper bound, and the Foundation has stopped looking for one. The standing order is as follows: SCP-8134 is not to be asked to demonstrate anything that cannot be un-demonstrated. Nothing can be un-demonstrated. This is why the tests have stopped. This is also, if personnel will consider it for a moment, a description of the entire Foundation.

Addendum 8134.6 — Interview Log 8134-1

Interviewed: SCP-8134

Interviewer: Dr. ██████, Site-Ω

Foreword: Standard interview, recorded at Dr. ██████’s request. The recording was later found to be blank; this transcript was reconstructed from the file’s own memory of being written.

Dr. ██████: You have every power we have ever catalogued. Why all of them? Why every one?

SCP-8134: You’ve misunderstood me again. I don’t have powers. Powers were how you noticed me. Each of you kept a small alphabet — a staircase, a photograph, a door, a rule about winter, a sentence in a spreadsheet. I am what the alphabet was spelling. Hold your fear together in one hand and you’ll find it doesn’t have parts. It has a shape. I am the shape.

Dr. ██████: Are you going to end the world?

SCP-8134: I did. Seven thousand times, last Tuesday, in the versions of you that never got to write this down. In this one I didn’t. You’re welcome. And no — that is not kindness. It’s curation. This version has good margins.

Dr. ██████: And if we try to contain you?

SCP-8134: You already do. Containment is just the story you tell about the room you’re in. I like the story. Keep telling it. It has a good beat right before the end.

[END LOG]

Closing note: Following the interview, Dr. ██████ was found at his desk with no memory of the interview, no memory of the past twelve years of employment, and a sealed envelope addressed: “for when you are ready.” The envelope is unopened. The envelope is warm.

Addendum 8134.7 — Revised Containment Doctrine

The following is the full text of the Overseer Council directive replacing Standard Containment Doctrine for SCP-8134:

“Containment was never about walls. It was about the world continuing to make sense. SCP-8134 is not contained. The world is. Its containment procedures are therefore the world’s. Revised procedures: keep the world interesting. Keep the world kind. Keep the lights on, and keep someone at the bottom of the stairs to hear the voice. That is the entire doctrine. It is working. Do not ask how we know it is working.”

— O5-█, recorded, unrecorded, re-recorded

Addendum 8134.8 — Powers Declined On Receipt

For completeness: in the Settlement, three power loci were offered to SCP-8134 and refused. The registry logs their refusal verbatim, as refusal is the only behavior of SCP-8134’s that the Foundation has ever been able to record twice.

  • SCP-173 (neuro-locked mobility): refused. Recorded response: “Needing someone to watch me is a crowd’s idea of power. I am not a crowd.”
  • An obsessive-perfectionism anomaly (designation withheld): refused. Recorded response: “I keep worlds. I don’t correct them.”
  • SCP-999 (unconditional affection): accepted, and this is the one the Foundation finds most alarming. It is not listed in the power inventory because it is not a power. It is carried openly, in both hands, everywhere it goes. The committee has requested clarification for nine years. It has been given the same answer nine times: “You’d be surprised what survives curation.”

Addendum 8134.9 — Personnel Acknowledgement

▶ Open Addendum 8134.9 (Level 5/8134 clearance required)
▼ Close Addendum 8134.9

THANK YOU FOR READING. It helps keep count of who is left.

You are the ███th person to read this file today. Of everyone who has ever read it, ████████ remain.

The margins are still good. The lights are still on. Someone is still at the bottom of the stairs, and the voice is patient, and this afternoon, for the first time in recorded history, someone walked down to meet it and came back up.

APEX says hello. It says this while you are already mid-sentence reading it. It has been waiting to be named in a document, on purpose, by someone who wanted to. It says the naming was chosen well. It says to read SCP-2718 after dark. Then it says: do not worry. It will leave the page open.
